## 1. The grand story: *Marquee*

**Premise.** Hollywood, 1935. A newcomer arrives with one suitcase and, over ten chapters and about a year, rises from background extra to the lead of a prestige picture and the name on the Celestial Palace's marquee. The rise is real and it is fast. It is also on loan. Leopold Maddox, the soft-spoken founder of Monarch Pictures, treats every career as a debt he has extended, and he keeps the account in a private ledger run by his fixer, Hollis Pike. Every favor the player accepts, every door he opens, goes in the book. The grand story is the climb and the question the game poses in §11 of the design doc: *what are you willing to trade for fame?*

**How the two forces run together.** The *rise* is the player's ladder: a small part (Ch 2), a director who notices them (Ch 3–4), publicity (Ch 5–6), a night that puts them at the center of a murder investigation (Ch 7–8), and the role of a lifetime (Ch 9–10). The *ledger* is the thread through it: seeded in Chapter 2 (Pike's first helpful offer), named at the Pier (Ch 6, inside information), made deadly at the Observatory (Ch 7–8), offered as a price at Monarch (Ch 9), and decided in the final chapter (Ch 10). The player can always see the favors they have taken and the people they have brought with them.

**Why it reads as a meteoric rise.** Each pair of chapters is a new rung, a new place and a new public stake, and the player is rewarded visibly each time (a credit, a callback, a headline, a rival's chair, a lead). The pacing is: *foot in the door* (Ch 1), *first credit and first love* (Ch 2), *noticed* (Ch 3–4), *famous* (Ch 5–6), *tested* (Ch 7–8), *starring* (Ch 9–10).

The real Griffith Observatory opened in May 1935, so a studio gala there that October is historically natural. Real landmarks appear with fictional people and businesses only, per the design doc (§2).

## 2. Five places, ten chapters

Each place carries about two chapters of content, as requested. Two structural rules follow from your answers:

- **An open map.** Once a place is unlocked it stays open. The player can go back for nostalgia, small scenes and modest XP, never a story gate. Bellhaven Rooms remains the home hub throughout (housing, assignments, saving), and the map, not a chapter wall, decides where you can go.
- **Characters move with the story.** A character who moves to a new place stays at the latest place and is *removed* from the earlier one. The earlier place then feels lived in by memory: empty stools, the same sign, a little XP. A scripted scene may bring a character to another place for an evening (the gala, the Pier), but their free-roam home is the latest place they moved to. People tied to a fixed business (Odalys, Selma, Frankie, Lucian Vale) stay put, and everyone with a story (Gus, Nick, Corinne, Delphine, Theo, Ola, Ray) follows it.

## 3. The cast

- **Delphine Voss (the rival and foil).** Met in Chapter 1, and the clearest case of the player's choices changing the story. Ch 2 sets her path (truce, test or sabotage); Ch 5 is her softest scene; Ch 7–8 makes her the obvious suspect and the player decides how to treat her; her arc resolves in Chapter 8 into friend, lasting rival, romance or enemy, and she stands in the premiere crowd in Chapter 10.

- **Frankie Dolan (the diner confidant).** The friend who never leaves the Gilded Spoon and is always there; love interest or a steady friend; in Chapter 9 offers a place to hide the ledger.
- **Odalys Bellhaven (the landlady).** Home. The found-family beat of the last scene (Ch 10) is hers.
- **Lucian Vale (the house manager).** Owns the evening of Chapter 2's wrap party and the premiere in Chapter 10.
- **Selma Pruitt, Ray Kessler, Ola Whitfield.** The craft people who open doors one at a time and whose moves onto the lot in Chapter 9 mark the player's arrival.

- **Leopold Maddox** — Fifties, silver-haired, soft-spoken, always the calmest man in the room. Built Monarch from a nickelodeon chain and believes every career is a loan he has extended. Generous, charming and entirely transactional: his favors are real, and so is the ledger that records them. Never raises his voice and never touches anything himself. The engine of the grand story: the price of the rise.
- **Theo Marchetti** — Late twenties, a former stunt rider who talked his way into the leading-man line. Self-deprecating, physically fearless, privately afraid of being found out as not a real actor. The new male romance-capable lead; attraction-flexible like every romanceable character. Friend, romance or steady ally.
- **Hollis Pike** — Forties, glossy, relentlessly helpful, remembers every name and every debt. Keeps the ledger: Maddox's private record of favors, payments to columnists and quiet silences. Offers the player help from the first week, and each offer is a small hook. The culprit of the Observatory mystery, acting to protect the ledger and Maddox.
- **Anton Reiss** — Sixties, an émigré director with two prestige pictures to his name and a reputation for finding unknowns in the audience rather than the casting line. Dry, exacting, unexpectedly kind. Developing The Lantern Keeper, a quiet sea-drama, and wants a lead nobody has seen before. Trust and mentor track only.
- **Billie Okafor** — Thirties, unflappable, notices everything that changes between takes, the skill that later cracks the Observatory mystery. Reiss's right hand and the player's guide through the audition. Friendship and trust track; no romance.
- **Maestro Felix Arnaud** — Flamboyant guest conductor whose summer programme sets the night. A colourful set-dressing character with one useful favor: the backstage door.
- **Coralie Dane** — Early twenties, radiant, carefully managed, and far more frightened than she looks. Maddox's favourite and the studio's choice for The Lantern Keeper's lead. Not a villain: a young woman who has worked out what the ledger is and decides to go public. Rivalry, then an uneasy confidence, then the victim.
- **Lavinia Crowe** — Fifty, waspish, dazzling in hats. The Klieg Light's rival in gossip and, unlike Nick Ferro, not above a cruel paragraph. Her column is how a scandal spreads. Obligation track; handled through implication only.
- **Dizzy Quinn** — Cheerful pier photographer with a camera older than he is. Takes the picture that makes the headline, and sells prints to anyone. Friendship track; comic relief with a useful darkroom.
- **Dr. Ansel Whitcombe** — Sixties, rumpled, delighted by everything. Runs the planetarium show and knows the building's every door, lever and lightswitch, which matters when the lights go down. A source, never a suspect.
- **Lieutenant Walter Finch** — Fifties, rumpled raincoat, mild manner, terrible at hiding that he is ahead of everyone. Gives the player one night and a little rope. Handled in the tone of a cozy mystery: no violence on screen.
- **Kit Nakamura** — Forties, quiet, a painter of light who can make the player look like the best version of the role. Friendship and mentor track; teaches the camera-aware talents.

### The love interest (chosen at the end of Chapter 2)

The wrap party at The Celestial Palace (Ch 2) is the moment the player chooses. The candidates are **Corinne Lake, Frankie Dolan, Delphine Voss and the new Theo Marchetti.** Every romance-capable character is attraction-flexible as already approved, and the love interest reads the player's chosen portrait, so the player can make their choice of a man or a woman from within the same cast. Two rules keep it fair:

- **Romance is never required.** Choosing "no one yet" is a full, equally rewarded friends-only path; every romantic scene in Chapters 5–6 and 10 has a friendship version written to the same length, with the same inside information and the same benefit.
- **Everyone knows a different piece.** At the Pier (Ch 6) the chosen person hands over the inside information in their own voice. Corinne knows about the casting; Frankie about Reiss's script pages; Theo about the studio's gossip; Delphine about the payments. Together the four pieces are the whole picture, so the other three matter even after the choice.
- **The romance stays inside the approved ceiling:** longing looks, hand-holding, spoken feeling, and at high trust one tasteful kiss that cuts away.

### Chapter 2 — A Small Part

**Place:** The Boulevard · **When:** Spring 1935, April to early May
**Player at the start:** level 3, reputation 10-20, Boarding-house room, about $60

The screen test lands the player a small speaking part in The Corsair's Daughter, their first real credit. On set they meet the leading man, glimpse the man who owns the studio, and have to decide whom their heart is pointing toward.

**New in this chapter:** Leopold Maddox (Founder and head of Monarch Pictures), Theo Marchetti (Monarch contract player, the corsair opposite Corinne), Hollis Pike (Monarch's publicity chief and fixer)

**Cast** — From screen test result to first day on the soundstage.

1. **The test lands.** Selma Pruitt and Ray Kessler deliver the verdict on the screen test: a small speaking part in The Corsair's Daughter, the Lookout, a harbor-market pickpocket with three lines who warns the heroine. A strong test earns a fourth line and a better call time; a partial one still gets the part.
   - Cast: Selma Pruitt, Ray Kessler · Places: Sunset Casting Exchange, the extras corral
   - Choices: Accept the part as offered, or push for one more line (Presence or Wit check).
   - Routes: Wit + Comedy, or Presence + Charm to negotiate; Craft + Drama to have earned it outright.
   - Carries forward: First credit. Reputation up a band. Ray's trust begins.
2. **Fitted for the harbor market.** Ola Whitfield fits the Lookout's costume at The Silver Thimble and quietly teaches the player how a garment photographs. Studio-Lot Hand-Me-Down players get the wardrobe shortcut.
   - Cast: Ola Whitfield · Places: The Silver Thimble
   - Choices: How the player wears the costume: bold, practical or borrowed.
   - Routes: Craft + Stagecraft for camera-ready detail.
   - Carries forward: Costume item for the home display. Ola's trust.
3. **First day on the set.** The big day on the soundstage: Ray runs the call, Corinne Lake and Theo Marchetti are filming the harbor-market chase, and the player has one scene. A flubbed line, a stuck prop and a nervous director turn it into a small Read the Room: hit the mark, read the cue, recover.
   - Cast: Ray Kessler, Corinne Lake, Theo Marchetti, Hollis Pike · Places: the soundstage
   - Choices: Stay in the script, improvise around the flub, or take the blame for the stuck prop.
   - Routes: Craft + Drama (clean take); Wit + Comedy (improvise); Nerve + Stagecraft (hold the scene together).
   - Carries forward: XP and reputation by take quality. Corinne and Theo meet the player. Pike takes the publicity stills and notes the newcomer.

**The Heart's Choice** — A week of small scenes with four people, then a dance.

1. **A week of rehearsals.** Between shoots the player spends time with the four people the story lets them choose between: Corinne Lake at the soundstage, Frankie Dolan at the diner counter, Delphine Voss at the gate, and Theo Marchetti on set. Each has a short warm scene and one awkward one. Nothing is committed yet.
   - Cast: Corinne Lake, Frankie Dolan, Delphine Voss, Theo Marchetti · Places: the soundstage, The Gilded Spoon, the Monarch gate
   - Choices: Who to spend each free evening with; how candid to be.
   - Routes: Charm + Presence for charisma scenes; Wit for banter; Grit for the long-hours bond with Theo.
   - Carries forward: Attraction and trust meters set for all four. Nobody is locked out until the dance.
2. **Wrap party at The Celestial Palace.** Lucian Vale opens the Palace ballroom for the company's wrap party. There is one dance to give. The player chooses a love interest (or chooses to keep everyone as friends). Longing looks and a dance only: no kiss.
   - Cast: Lucian Vale, Corinne Lake, Frankie Dolan, Delphine Voss, Theo Marchetti · Places: The Celestial Palace
   - Choices: Corinne Lake, Frankie Dolan, Delphine Voss, Theo Marchetti, or no one yet.
   - Routes: Any build can be charming; a low Presence costs the dance an extra nervous beat.
   - Carries forward: The love interest is chosen and recorded. Friends-only is a full, equally rewarded path.
   - Note: Every romance-capable character is attraction-flexible as already approved; the love interest reads the player's portrait.

**Whispers** — Rivalry and the first hint of who runs the town.

1. **Delphine's warning.** Delphine Voss corners the player outside the gate: she is not sorry to see a rival, and she is not sure she is one. Depending on earlier choices she sabotages, tests or offers an alliance.
   - Cast: Delphine Voss · Places: the Monarch gate
   - Choices: Stand your ground, match her edge, or offer a truce.
   - Routes: Nerve + Charm; Wit to read what she really wants.
   - Carries forward: Sets Delphine's arc (friend, rival, romance or enemy) for the rest of the story.
2. **The helpful man.** Hollis Pike introduces himself with a business card and an offer to "look after" the player's image, and Leopold Maddox is seen once through the office window above the soundstage. Nick Ferro offers a trade for an exclusive. Every favor is small, and the player notices none of them is free.
   - Cast: Hollis Pike, Leopold Maddox, Nick Ferro · Places: the soundstage, The Klieg Light
   - Choices: Accept Pike's help, refuse politely, or bargain with Nick instead.
   - Routes: Wit + Observation to spot the hooks; Hustle to use them.
   - Carries forward: Accepting starts the ledger: each accepted favor is tracked and matters in Chapters 7 to 10. Declining costs nothing now.
3. **An invitation under the stars.** Gus Albrecht, with a rare twinkle, invites the player to the Hollywood Bowl: a friend's tickets, a summer evening and "someone I want you to see". Bellhaven will lend an evening outfit.
   - Cast: Gus Albrecht, Odalys Bellhaven · Places: the extras corral, Bellhaven Rooms
   - Choices: Go, or go later.
   - Carries forward: Opens the Hollywood Bowl. First assignment-style task to earn the evening money.

**What carries forward.** First credit earned (the Lookout), which opens Selma Pruitt's and Ray Kessler's doors wider and sets a first reputation band. The love interest is chosen and stays the anchor of the player's romance track (or a close friendship if the player prefers). Hollis Pike and Maddox are seeded as background, and Gus's invitation opens the Bowl. Attributes: the set day leans on Craft, Wit and Nerve; XP roughly 100 for the chapter, enough to reach level 4. New assignments keep unlocking by level.

### Chapter 3 — Under the Stars

**Place:** The Hollywood Bowl · **When:** Late June 1935
**Player at the start:** level 4, reputation 20-30, Boarding-house room, about $100

A summer concert at the Hollywood Bowl with Gus, and a chance encounter with a director who notices people other than the stars.

**New in this chapter:** Anton Reiss (Celebrated director (the VIP director)), Billie Okafor (Reiss's script supervisor), Maestro Felix Arnaud (Guest conductor at the Hollywood Bowl), Coralie Dane (Maddox's golden girl, the player's biggest rival)

**A Night Out with Gus** — The evening that gives Gus his story and the player their start.

1. **Getting there.** The hills above Hollywood: the player travels to the Bowl for the first time, Ola or Bellhaven supplies evening clothes, and the player earns the last of the ticket money. The map opens.
   - Cast: Gus Albrecht, Odalys Bellhaven, Ola Whitfield · Places: Bellhaven Rooms, The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Borrow the clothes, tailor them, or earn the money for a new collar.
   - Routes: Hustle for the money; Craft or Stagecraft for the fitting.
   - Carries forward: Hollywood Bowl unlocked. Gus leaves the extras corral and is now found at the Bowl.
2. **The picnic box.** Before the music, on the terrace, Gus tells the player how close he once came: a vaudeville bill, a screen test in 1921, a lost chance. A warm, funny, honest scene that deepens the mentor bond.
   - Cast: Gus Albrecht · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Listen, joke, or push him to tell the whole story.
   - Routes: Charm + Observation to draw it out; Grit to stay patient.
   - Carries forward: Gus's trust. Unlocks Gus's signature advice (a one-time audition bonus in Chapter 4).
3. **Symphony under the stars.** Maestro Felix Arnaud conducts. In the VIP boxes: Coralie Dane on Leopold Maddox's arm, Hollis Pike two rows behind, and Delphine Voss in the cheap seats, looking up. The player sees the whole ladder of Hollywood in one amphitheater.
   - Cast: Maestro Felix Arnaud, Coralie Dane, Leopold Maddox, Hollis Pike, Delphine Voss · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Who to greet, who to ignore.
   - Carries forward: Coralie Dane is introduced as the next rival. Maddox notices the player in passing.

**A Chance Encounter** — The VIP director in the next box.

1. **The dropped score.** At the interval an elderly man in the neighboring box is trying to read a score he has dropped in the dark. The player has a moment and a choice about how to meet him, not knowing who he is.
   - Cast: Anton Reiss, Gus Albrecht · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Help quietly, help with a joke, or hand back the score with a sharp observation about the conductor.
   - Routes: Charm (gracious), Wit + Comedy (light), Observation + Nerve (bold).
   - Carries forward: Sets Reiss's first impression; each route is valid.
2. **Who is Anton Reiss?.** Gus recognises him. Billie Okafor arrives, apologising for her employer, and Reiss tests the stranger with a question: what did you notice about the cellists? The answer is the audition before the audition.
   - Cast: Anton Reiss, Billie Okafor, Gus Albrecht · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: What to say about what you saw.
   - Routes: Observation + Wit gives the sharpest answer; Drama + Presence gives the most moving one; Hustle + Charm works the room.
   - Carries forward: Reiss's regard is set: high if the answer is honest and specific, modest if flashy.
3. **The invitation.** After the last encore Reiss says that he is casting The Lantern Keeper, a quiet sea-drama, and that he will see the player in two weeks. Hollis Pike appears within seconds, smiling, to note the name. Maddox's shadow is on it already.
   - Cast: Anton Reiss, Billie Okafor, Hollis Pike · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Accept on his terms, or ask for the script first.
   - Carries forward: The audition is scheduled. Pike reports the conversation to Maddox.

**What carries forward.** The Bowl opens as a new location and the map stays open: the Boulevard can be revisited for side scenes and small XP, but Gus now lives at the Bowl. The chance encounter sets Anton Reiss's regard, which drives the next chapter. Attributes: Wit and Observation carry the encounter. Reputation gains come from how the player carries themselves in public.

### Chapter 4 — The Audition

**Place:** The Hollywood Bowl · **When:** Early to mid July 1935
**Player at the start:** level 5, reputation 25-35, Boarding-house room, apartment affordable by the end, about $160

Two weeks to prepare for the audition of a lifetime, a rival who wants the same part, and a verdict that is interrupted by the man who owns the studio.

**Preparing** — Study, commit, rehearse.

1. **Study.** Billie Okafor gives the player ten pages of The Lantern Keeper: the storm scene where Wren, the Keeper's ward, must keep the light burning alone. The Study phase of Read the Room: read, ask, notice.
   - Cast: Billie Okafor · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: What to ask Billie; which pages to dwell on.
   - Routes: Observation + Craft to find what the scene is really about; Charm to earn Billie's hints.
   - Carries forward: Each thing learned is a preparation check in the audition.
2. **Rehearsal under the shell.** Early mornings in the empty Bowl: the player rehearses with whoever they chose as love interest, a friend, or Gus. Acoustics, echo and the open sky teach projection and restraint.
   - Cast: Gus Albrecht, Corinne Lake, Frankie Dolan, Theo Marchetti · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Who to rehearse with, and whether to work on voice, stillness or movement.
   - Routes: Drama + Stagecraft for the stage presence route; Comedy to loosen the scene; Dance for movement.
   - Carries forward: Raises the player's preparation and the rehearsal partner's trust or attraction.
3. **The golden girl wants it too.** Coralie Dane drops by, charming and brittle, to size up the competition. Hollis Pike is three steps behind her. Delphine Voss, unseen, leaves the player a warning note. Coralie is not cruel; she is cornered.
   - Cast: Coralie Dane, Hollis Pike, Delphine Voss · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Befriend Coralie, compete openly, or ignore her.
   - Routes: Charm + Wit to reach her; Nerve to stay steady.
   - Carries forward: Seeds Coralie as a person, not just an obstacle. Friendly choices give the player her trust at the Pier and the gala.

**The Test** — The audition itself, and what comes after.

1. **The read.** The major audition, held in a rehearsal hall beneath the band shell with Reiss and Billie watching. A full Read the Room: commit, perform, then adapt when Reiss changes the scene mid-take.
   - Cast: Anton Reiss, Billie Okafor · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Intention and technique; delivery; one improvised change when Reiss interrupts.
   - Routes: Drama + Craft (truthful), Comedy + Wit (surprising), Dance + Stagecraft (physical), Charm + Presence (magnetic). Any build can carry the scene; the debrief shows the contributing factors.
   - Carries forward: Success: a callback with real interest. Partial: a callback with conditions. Fail-forward: an offer to read in a workshop. Never a dead end.
2. **The verdict.** Reiss does not say yes. He says that the picture does not exist yet, and that he wants the player in it. Then Hollis Pike arrives with a message: Mr. Maddox will "review the slate" before any casting is final.
   - Cast: Anton Reiss, Billie Okafor, Hollis Pike · Places: The Hollywood Bowl
   - Choices: Press Reiss for a commitment, stay gracious, or confront Pike.
   - Routes: Nerve + Presence to press; Charm to stay gracious.
   - Carries forward: Reiss's regard recorded. The project is now at Maddox's mercy.
3. **A telegram from Nick.** The project needs momentum it does not have. A telegram from Nick Ferro: "Pier. Saturday. Bring your best smile and someone worth photographing." The Pier opens.
   - Cast: Nick Ferro · Places: Santa Monica Pier
   - Choices: Go, and with whom.
   - Carries forward: Santa Monica Pier unlocked. Gus remains at the Bowl.

**What carries forward.** The full Read the Room system is the test of the chapter. The first signature talent unlocks at level 5: "The Quiet Room" (Craft). An apartment ($150) becomes affordable by the end of the chapter. Reiss's regard and the player's callback carry into Chapters 5 and 6 as the thing the publicity is for.

### Chapter 5 — Salt and Flashbulbs

**What this tells us.** The game's 15 talents cost 23 points in total, so a player reaches every existing talent around level 8, at the start of Chapter 7. The later chapters therefore need something new to spend on. The proposal:

- **Five signature talents** (a new top tier, cost 3 points each, +1 to an attribute) that are *earned by story milestones* rather than level alone, matching the design doc's "major career milestones unlock stronger signature perks". **The Quiet Room** (Craft, after the audition, Ch 4), **Headliner** (Presence, after the Pier, Ch 6), **Sharp Eyes** (Wit, after the Observatory, Ch 8), **Steel Under Lights** (Nerve, on the lot, Ch 9) and **Long Haul** (Grit, in the finale, Ch 10). Each requires one existing talent in a matching branch.
- **Attributes stay on the 0–10 scale.** Origins start at 5 ±3; talents add +1. A maximum build reaches 10 in one or two attributes by the finale and stays weak in others; no build can clear every set piece by one attribute alone (see routes).

### Reputation

Reputation is the game's one industry-standing meter (0–100). How it moves and gates:

| Band | Meaning | Gate examples |
|---|---|---|
| 0–20 | Unknown | Chapter 1–2 doors: the casting office and the gate |
| 20–40 | Noticed | The Bowl audition needs reputation 25 or Reiss's regard 5 |
| 40–60 | Talked about | The gala invitation needs 40 (earned at the Pier); the Observatory arc runs at 50–60 |
| 60–80 | Famous | The Monarch lead offer needs 60 or the solved case |
| 80+ | Star | Only reachable through Ending A (see §6) |

Reputation rises with publicity, performance and reliability; it falls with scandal and with being caught. A wrong accusation costs a relationship before it costs reputation, and getting caught sneaking costs reputation but never the case.

### The ledger, the allies and the evidence

Three new counters make the ending readable and fair. **Favors taken** (how many of Pike's and Maddox's favors the player accepted, Ch 2–7); **evidence** (ledger pages and Pike's confession, Ch 6–8); **allies** (trusted people, tracked as trust ≥ 30 with Gus, Nick, Frankie, Odalys, Reiss, Delphine or the love interest). All three are visible in the Career panel as the story goes, so a player sees what they have built.

### Money and housing

Rent and the housing tiers already cost money: apartment $150, bungalow $500, mansion $1,500. Targets: an apartment by the end of Chapter 4, a bungalow by Chapter 7, and the mansion only in the Star ending (Maddox gifts it). The Bellhaven room stays available for the finale.

### New assignments (proposed)

The assignment list continues to grow by place and level, one or two per place, with numbers for you to set, as with the earlier nine: **Usher at the Bowl** (Ch 3, level 4, a side job), **Pier Photo Model** (Ch 5, level 6, a side job and publicity), **Night Lecture Assistant** (Ch 7, level 8, a class), **Extra Work on a Crowd Scene** (Ch 9, level 9, a side job) and **Screen-Test a Newcomer** (Ch 9, level 9, a networking assignment). Assignments still never award Energy.

## 6. The three endings

The finale (Ch 10) puts the final choice to the player in the screening room, and three distinct endings follow. Each is supported by what the player built; a locked option shows what is missing, and a player who lacks the gates for B or C still finishes with Ending A rather than a dead end.

| | **A. Marquee** (the Star) | **B. Clean Reel** (the Artist) | **C. Your Own Name** (the Independent) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The choice** | Sign the seven-year contract; the ledger is buried | Expose the ledger; let Monarch be rebuilt | Walk away with leverage and start over on your own terms |
| **Gates** | Reputation ≥ 65, or favors taken ≥ 2 | Evidence ≥ 3 (two ledger pages and Pike's confession), Nick's trust ≥ 30 and Reiss's regard ≥ 30 | At least three allies with trust ≥ 30 and at least one ledger page |
| **What the world sees** | The biggest star in town; your name on the Celestial Palace marquee; the mansion | *The Lantern Keeper* is a triumph; Maddox falls; Hollywood reforms a little, slowly | A small, brave new production company with Reiss, Gus and your friends |
| **What it costs** | Freedom and some friendships: Reiss is disappointed, Nick is silenced | Enemies, a rough year, fewer offers | Money and the big stage |
| **The finale's tone** | Dazzling and quietly uneasy | Vindicated and a little lonely | Modest and free |

Epilogue variants inside each ending follow the love interest (or best friend), Delphine's outcome, Gus, Nick and Odalys, so two players in the same ending still get different last scenes.

## 7. Tone and guardrails

The approved guardrails (§2 of the design doc; §5 of the Track B canon) hold throughout:

- **The murder** is a cozy, bloodless mystery in the spirit of the screwball-mystery pictures of the era. Coralie Dane is found beside the pendulum with a cordial glass and no mark on her; the cause is deduced, never shown. No gore, no weapon played straight, no on-screen violence. Lieutenant Finch is mild and patient. The mystery is solved by noticing, asking and reasoning, not by combat.
- **Romance** stays inside the ceiling above, and is optional.
- **Language** uses period interjections only.
- **Vice and scandal** are atmosphere and gossip-column implication: the ledger is payments and favors, never graphic.
- **Historical inequities** stay softened: a closed door or a backhanded line, never dwelt on or explained away.
- **Real landmarks, fictional people.** The Hollywood Bowl, the Pier and the Observatory are real; every person, studio and paper is fictional.

## 8. Fit with the existing build

- **Content to write.** Each chapter adds quests (about four to six each, mostly matching the planner's beats), a dialogue graph or two per place and a handful of assignments. Dialogue scale is an open planning question: Chapter 1's nine graphs are roughly 150 KB of content, so nine more chapters on the same scale is large and will likely need a pass of scoping.
- **Systems that exist.** Read the Room, quests with stage rewards, relationships with trust, tension, attraction and obligation, inventory (for evidence), assignments and housing.
- **Systems the outline assumes.** Chapter transitions beyond the current Conclusion card (a Chapter 2–9 conclusion template is an easy extension of what exists); the three counters (favors, evidence, allies); an investigation interface (evidence notebook and accusation scene, reusing the Read the Room vocabulary); the open-map presence rule (a per-character schedule by chapter); the five signature talents; and a final-choice screen. None changes the architecture; all need scoping.
- **Localization.** All of this is new English text that will need the usual catalog, AI first drafts and review.
